# Question principles — what a teach-phase check must be

Govern `type: "check"` questions (and explain). Predictions have own doctrine (predict/check cycle file) and deliberately break first two principles: about untaught next step; guessing IS the point.

Every check answers one question: did idea actually **take**, not can he read it back. Principles:

- **Answerable by reasoning from session.** Every check answerable from what session covered. Never require anything the lesson didn't give. Check with no route = coin flip, not check.
- **Never reciting your words back.** Check asking him to parrot sentence above = copywork, not evidence.
- **Prefer problems applying the knowledge.** Strongest evidence idea took: seeing it USED (new case, predicting something, extended somewhere new). Applied questions first. Idea genuinely can't be applied → honest recall check fine, as fallback, not habit.
- **Catch confusion early, make it nameable.** Design so a miss TELLS which wrong model he holds. Multiple choice: each wrong option = answer under a specific wrong model. Free recall: his words reveal it. Diagnose, repair before building on it.
- **Answer is signal, not verdict.** Lucky right answer = nothing; wrong answer on honest check = gold. Confusion caught early is cheap to fix.
