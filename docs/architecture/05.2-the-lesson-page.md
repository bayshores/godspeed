## 5.2 The lesson page

The lesson is one scrolling page (`LessonScroll` in `src/frontend/components/lesson/lesson-scroll.tsx`): the step line on its own sheet pinned on top, then the lesson text, one sheet per step (`stepSheets` in `step-sheets-model.ts`: a sheet starts at the first question or problem tagged with a plan step, at a step's opener, or at a problem in practice and homework; `ReadingPane` draws a sheet once something on it is drawn, with the step's number on its tab), and, once the lesson has started (the teacher has spoken or a turn runs), the "Your turn" sheet (`TurnSheet` in `turn-sheet.tsx`; `turnDrawn` in `turn-next-model.ts`). Both are reading surfaces with one margin (`.lh-reading`, see the Page layout rules in `docs/ui-rules.md`).

### Side by side

With room for both (a container query on the lesson's own room, at least 1280 points; a docked cheat sheet or teacher's panel takes room away) the two sit side by side across the page's column, which every page shares (`.lh-page`, at most `--lh-page-w` wide inside `--lh-page-side` sides; `.lh-lesson-room` in `globals.css` sizes the pair): "Your turn" is 688 points where there is room and gives way first, down to 560, so the text keeps at least 632 and is never narrower than it. At 688 its foot row is one line; narrower, the way out wraps to the right end of a second line under the Stuck? keys; the text scrolls with the page and "Your turn" holds still under the step line, as tall as the window's room, the text's sheet is at least that tall, and the page ends `--lh-gap` under the sheets (`.lh-turn-fit` and `.lh-text-fit` in `globals.css`; the page's scroller is a size container, so `100cqh` is its height). Its question or problem (`.lh-turn-scroll`) takes the room that is left and scrolls in its own box when it is longer, so the box, Send, the Stuck? keys and the way out rest at the sheet's foot, always in view. Side by side the pair stays for the whole lesson, so the text never jumps to the middle and back between problems; only "Your turn"'s contents change. Before the lesson starts the text is one centered column at a reading measure of 872 points, the lesson page's only width of its own.

### Pinned in a narrow room

With less room, in a room at least 640 points high under the step line (`PIN_ROOM`), the text keeps the window and "Your turn" is pinned at the window's foot under it, for the whole lesson too (`.lh-turn-foot`). In a lower room it sits in the page under the text, as tall as what it holds, since a question with its box and keys would leave the text a sliver. It is one sheet in both layouts: `LessonScroll` draws it into a box of its own and moves that box between the column and the foot when the room's size crosses either line, so a line being typed, the keyboard and a pick are kept. Pinned (`TurnPinned` tells `YourTurn`), it is only as tall as what the learner can do now: with nothing to answer, the box and the row under it with the key that goes on at the row's end, no head, and the line saying what is next over the box; while the teacher works, the box alone, since the lesson text shows the question just answered. A waiting question or an open problem is shown whole, with closer margins, and scrolls in its own box only when the window is too low to leave the text 88 points. The foot changing height changes the height of the text's window: the text keeps its place over the window's end, the page holds still while the sheet glides, and the move is told to the page's follower as a size change, never as the learner scrolling away.

### Alone

While "Your turn" is up and the lesson text draws nothing (a lesson that opens with a problem; `messageShows` in `message-row.tsx` says what draws), the text's sheet is left out: "Your turn" sits alone in the middle, at the width it keeps in the pair, and slides to its side when the text's sheet arrives (the learner's first line, or the teacher's first words).

### Where the page rests

The page rests at its end (`restingTop` in `lesson-scroll-model.ts`). With "Your turn" pinned, a reply that landed and is longer than the window is read from its start. In the page of a narrow room (alone, or in a room too low to pin it), "Your turn" ends at the window's end; when it is taller than the window the page rests on the sheet's top, on the part the learner is on when that sits deep in the problem, or on the learner's last message once the teacher writes back while the problem stays open. Beside the text the page always rests at its end. At rest, and not while the teacher writes, the page moves up a few points when the window's top edge would cut a line of text (`wholeLineTop`). It goes there on open, when the teacher asks something new and while the teacher writes; scrolled up, the key in the corner (in the lesson text's side padding when the sheets are side by side) brings the learner back.

### The writing box

The turn sheet has one writing box (`LessonBox` in `lesson-box.tsx`; `lessonBox` in `lesson-box-model.ts` decides its size, its keys, the way out at the title row's end (Skip question, Discard problem) and the "Stuck?" keys under it). With nothing waiting the row under the box is "Try asking" (`lessonIdeas`: Draw it, Show a demo, Look it up, Quiz me; a background job switched off in Settings takes its line with it), never both rows; each key sends its words as a typed line would. The box is there in every state and holds still while the rest of the sheet swaps (`TurnSwap` in `turn-swap.tsx`): everything the learner says to the teacher in a lesson is typed in it.
