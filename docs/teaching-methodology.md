# The theory of learning and teaching

The theory godspeed teaches by. The agent prompts in `crates/agent/prompts/` are a compressed form of it, so read this before you change how the teacher teaches.

## 1. Two knowledge states

A session is bounded by two states:

- **Current understanding** is everything the learner currently holds. What a learner *holds* is, by definition, what they accept as obviously true. The current state is the set of held propositions: accepted, self-evident, safe to build on.
- **Goal understanding** is the target of the session: what the learner wants to hold that they don't yet. It is often hard for the learner to articulate, and pinning it down is part of defining it.

Measuring defines both states precisely. It brackets the current understanding (what is held, and where it ends) and makes the goal concrete. Teaching then moves the learner from the first state to the second.

In a study session the teacher gets each topic's standing from the learner's past answers. In an older teach lesson a prober measures first, before the teacher starts.

## 2. Learning is the goal feeling obviously true

The end of a session is not recall, not being able to answer questions, and not coverage. It is that the **goal understanding feels obviously true** to the learner. The strength of understanding is measured in self-evidence. A fact that can be recited but doesn't feel self-evident is not learned. It is memorized, and memorized facts rot.

This is also how retention works. The brain commits fully to a fact only when it is safe to lock in (nothing more fundamental might later contradict it) and when it is connected, held in place by its relations and not by rehearsal. Feeling obviously true *is* the felt experience of both conditions being met.

## 3. The click: loose facts collapsing into generators

Learning has a payoff moment, and it is not the arrival of the final fact. The click is what happens to knowledge already held. Loose, lonely facts, known to be true but held as "that's just how it is", collapse into a structure generated by a few base facts. Each loose node is seen to follow from something more fundamental, and the pile becomes a derivation.

This is knowing why instead of knowing that: the strongest form of understanding, and the clearest felt evidence that structure has been built. The goal understanding (Section 2) is the end state of the same thing. It doesn't land as another loose fact. It is obvious because the structure generates it.

## 4. Knowledge is structure

Understanding is a graph: **propositions as nodes, connections as edges**. The connections between facts, and the reasoning steps that lead from one fact to another, are themselves facts. They can be stated, examined and held.

In the learner's mind, a new proposition that connects to nothing is fragile, and a proposition nested inside a structure is self-preserving. All of teaching is the construction of this structure.

## 5. Teaching is asserting facts in an order, one reasoning step at a time

The atomic act of teaching is exposing the learner to a fact. The whole of a lesson is **facts, in the right order, each presented in the clearest possible way.**

The order follows one rule: **move one reasoning step at a time.** Start from what the learner holds, which is obviously true by definition (Section 1). Attach each new fact to what is already held by a *single* reasoning step. A single step is small enough to be understood at once, and because it follows from what is held, the new fact inherits that obviousness. The whole session is a chain of small "of course" moments, ending at the goal.

Small steps also make errors surface at once. A gap or misconception shows itself the moment it is stepped on, not three nodes later when everything built on it is corrupted. Because the step was small, the failure is precisely localized. Strengthen or repair the weak spot *before continuing*, and never build on anything not yet held as obviously true.

## 6. Foundations

Some propositions the lesson needs cannot be derived from anything the learner holds. They need **foundational nodes**: the bottom of the dependency tree. A foundational fact must be accepted *as-is*, so foundations should be **unconditionally true**: simple enough to take at face value, with no caveats or hedging.

Foundations can also be **motivated** by a problem, a question or a condition that demands the fact. Motivation is not derivation. It eases acceptance without making the fact follow, but it works the same way: a fact that answers a felt question is easier to hold as self-evident than one that arrives from nowhere.

Derivation and foundation are the two placement moves. Derive when the lesson can reach the fact from what is held, and place a foundation when it can't. In the prompts these are Principle ii and Principle i (`teacher/05-method.md`).

## 7. Optimal learning is two independent optimizations

### 7a. The learner's effort goes into the material

The learner has finite mental effort per session. Optimize its **proportion**, not its amount.

- Maximize the share that goes into direct contact with the material: following steps, answering checks, holding the new facts.
- Minimize everything else, which is logistics: figuring out what to learn, in what order and from what source, researching, fact-checking, planning, finding resources and judging reliability.

The point is *not* to reduce the learner's effort. Struggle with the material is how it locks in. The point is that all of it goes into the material. The system does the logistics, out of band or silently, and the learner never does.

### 7b. The minimal tree

The lesson crosses a dependency structure: facts that require other facts. The optimization is **the minimal tree**, the smallest set of propositions, in the smallest derivation depth, that leads from the current understanding to the goal.

- Nothing the learner already holds is taught again. Measuring prunes those branches.
- Nothing off the path to the goal is taught, however interesting. Coverage is not the goal.
- The route is searched in planning, not improvised during teaching. Planning is the highest-leverage work in the session, and the quality of the lesson is decided there. Searching means scoping the field, finding what the goal actually depends on, pruning what is held and choosing the route.

The plan the learner sees is a **lower-resolution view** of this tree: a structural map of the lesson at a coarse level, not the literal reasoning steps. Plan nodes do not match reasoning steps or turns of teaching one to one. The reasoning step is the fine unit crossed in teaching, and the plan tree is the landscape it crosses. The plan is the map, not the road, so the lesson is never divided into "Node N" chapters.

## 8. Presentation: the clearest form of each step

Each reasoning step should be presented in the clearest, most articulate form, so the new understanding goes in as smoothly as possible and each step lands as self-evident. The presentation is chosen **per step**, as a means to clarity and never as a ritual.

- Some steps are best stated explicitly. Unconditional truths are often best asserted plainly as facts ("this is true; take it").
- Most connective tissue is better woven into the flow than announced. A derivation that says "now I will establish X, and then connect it to Y" makes the learner process the *lesson's choreography* instead of the material, which is logistics the theory says to remove. The reasoning should carry itself, each fact arriving as the obvious consequence of the last.
- The failure this corrects is not explicitness but **verbosity**: performing the method (labeled segments, restated structure, narrating the method) instead of doing the work. Every sentence either installs understanding or is overhead, and overhead must earn its place.

The measure of a step's presentation is one question: *does this maximize the learner's absorption?*

## 9. Checks after every reasoning step

Every reasoning step ends with a check: a graded question that verifies the step landed. This is the error-catching mechanism of Section 5. A step that didn't land is found the moment it is stepped on, located by the check, and strengthened before anything is built on it. Never teach several steps and then check once.

The checks set the session's rhythm. A prediction question can come before a step, and each check pauses the lesson for the learner's answer, so teaching moves in a rhythm of predict, step, check, then repair or continue.

## 10. What this asks of the system

- Measuring must produce precise states: what is held, with its edges, and a concrete goal. Its precision is the ceiling on planning quality (Section 1).
- Planning must be real work, and the plan shown to the learner is the visible evidence of that search (Section 7b).
- Teaching moves in single steps, each checked and repaired before the next (Sections 5 and 9).
- The plan tree's resolution must not leak into the lesson (Section 7b).
- Delivery is efficient by default, explicit only where it helps absorption (Section 8).
- The system owns the logistics (Section 7a).
